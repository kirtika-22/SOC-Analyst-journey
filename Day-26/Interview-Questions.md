# Day 26 — SOC Documentation & Investigation Templates

## 1. Why are SOC templates used?

SOC templates provide a standardized way to document alerts, incidents, evidence, actions, escalation, and handovers.

They improve consistency and make investigations easier to track and audit.

---

## 2. What is an Alert Triage Template?

An Alert Triage Template is used to document the initial investigation of an alert.

It records the alert details, evidence, findings, severity, and the analyst's decision to close, monitor, or escalate the alert.

---

## 3. What is an Incident Response Template?

An Incident Response Template documents the complete lifecycle of a security incident.

It can include:

- Detection
- Investigation
- Containment
- Eradication
- Recovery
- Lessons learned

---

## 4. What information should an incident report contain?

An incident report should contain:

- Incident summary
- Timeline
- Affected systems
- Evidence
- IOCs
- Root cause
- Impact
- Actions taken
- Current status
- Recommendations

---

## 5. What is a SOC handover?

A SOC handover is the process of transferring important investigation information from one SOC shift or analyst to another.

---

## 6. Why is SOC handover important?

It prevents:

- Missed actions
- Duplicate work
- Loss of investigation context

It also ensures that ongoing investigations continue smoothly.

---

## 7. What should be included in a SOC handover?

A handover should include:

- Active incidents
- Pending investigations
- Completed actions
- Pending actions
- Important IOCs
- Severity
- Case/ticket IDs
- Assigned team
- Recommended next steps

---

## 8. What is an Escalation Template?

An Escalation Template is used to transfer an investigation to a higher-level analyst or specialized security team.

For example:

**L1 SOC → L2 SOC → L3/IR Team**

It provides the next team with the necessary context and evidence.

---

## 9. When should an alert be escalated?

An alert should be escalated when:

- There is evidence of malicious activity
- The incident has significant impact
- Deeper investigation is required
- The analyst does not have sufficient authority or access
- The alert exceeds the analyst's scope

---

## 10. Why should an escalation contain evidence?

Evidence allows the next analyst or team to quickly understand the situation and make an informed decision.

It also prevents unnecessary investigation from being repeated.

---

# Scenario-Based Questions

## 11. You are ending your SOC shift and an investigation is still open. What will you do?

I will update the case with:

- Current status
- Findings
- Evidence
- Actions completed
- Pending actions
- Relevant IOCs
- Severity
- Recommended next steps

Then I will properly hand over the investigation to the next analyst.

---

## 12. How would you document a phishing incident?

I would document:

- Sender
- Recipient
- Subject
- Email headers
- URLs
- Attachments
- Domain/IP reputation
- Authentication results
- User interaction
- IOCs
- Investigation findings
- Final verdict
- Actions taken

---

## 13. An alert looks suspicious but you do not have enough evidence. What will you do?

I would collect additional evidence such as:

- Authentication logs
- Endpoint activity
- Network connections
- DNS activity
- Threat intelligence
- User activity

If I still cannot safely close the alert, I would escalate it with the evidence collected.

---

## 14. What is the difference between an alert and an incident?

An **alert** is a security notification generated by a security tool.

An **incident** is a confirmed or sufficiently suspected security event that requires investigation or response.

Example:

A SIEM detects multiple failed logins → Alert.

Evidence shows a successful unauthorized login followed by suspicious activity → Potential security incident.

---

## 15. Why should analysts avoid assumptions in documentation?

Because incorrect assumptions can mislead other analysts and result in incorrect response actions.

SOC documentation should clearly distinguish between:

- Confirmed facts
- Observations
- Hypotheses

---

## 16. What is a good SOC communication style?

SOC communication should be:

- Clear
- Concise
- Factual
- Professional
- Action-oriented

---

## 17. What happens if an analyst closes an incident without documenting evidence?

The SOC may lose the investigation trail.

This makes:

- Auditing difficult
- Future investigation difficult
- Incident reconstruction difficult
- Shift handover difficult

Therefore, important evidence and reasoning should be documented before closure.
